# Pre-Post Check

Deliverable: **a call and the full piece, ready to send.** The reply has four parts, in this order: one line with the call (send it, send with fixes, or hold) and how many facts were wrong or unsupported; the piece in full, ready to paste; a short table of what changed; at most three lines of things only the customer can confirm. Never a score, never tips, never a style edit. A clean piece gets "Send it" and comes back word for word, in full. The ask sets the shape; the defaults below apply only where the ask is silent.

Works in full with no Jinn connection: the user pastes the piece and anything they have (a product sheet, a price list, the offer terms), and you check it against those. Connected, each fact is looked up in the record of the product it names, so a figure true of one flavour is caught on another, and required legal lines come from the record.

Connect a brand at [jinn.works/products/agents](https://jinn.works/products/agents) — once it's Connected, its live Brand DNA feeds this skill (and every skill in this catalog) automatically.

Not this skill: writing the piece (`social-posts`, `email-sequence`, `landing-page-copy`, `product-listing-writer`, `ad-copy-variants`, `product-answers`), a red-line of long copy against the brand's voice, rules and strategy (`brand-guardrails-review`), whether copy sounds like the brand or like AI (`brand-voice-checker`), sorting a strategy doc's claims by the evidence behind them (`claim-provenance-checker`), building the list of claims the brand may make (`claims-registry`), text legibility on an image (`creative-contrast-qa`).

## Procedure (standalone — no Jinn connection needed)

### 1. Get the piece and know what each source is good for

You need the piece. Everything else is a source to check it against:

- **What the customer says in the ask, outside the piece** ("the sale ends Sunday", "it's new this month", "we're in 40 shops"): the standard. Words in the piece that say the same are never flagged and never changed, even where the record is silent or says otherwise; a clash with the record takes one confirm line at most.
- **The piece itself, whoever wrote it:** never proof of itself. Every fact in it is looked up.
- **An offer, code, event or send date the record has no field for:** stays as written. When the customer did not name it, it takes one confirm line ("Check: <the offer> is live"); it is never cut, because only the customer can know it.
- **The record** (a sheet the customer pasted, or the Jinn record): every field of every product, plus the brand text (mission, pillars, positioning, founding story, guidelines, disclaimers). A fact the record states anywhere, for this product or for the brand, is backed. Brand text is the only source for founding and history.
- **Review summaries:** what buyers say, never a customer quote.
- **General knowledge not about this brand** ("fibre is a carbohydrate"): not checked.

### 2. Let the ask set the shape

| The ask | Default |
|---------|---------|
| "Check this before it goes out" / "check the facts in this" | The four parts, every fact checked. |
| "Quick look" / "can this go?" | The four parts, the table only if something changed. |
| Several pieces (an email series, an ad set, a week of posts) | One call line per piece, then every piece in full, in order, changed or not, then one table with a "piece" column. A fault repeated across pieces is one row naming each piece. |
| A social post | Everything here, plus the post checks in step 6. |
| One piece for several platforms or channels | One version per channel where they differ. |
| A screenshot, an image ad, a sell sheet as a picture | Every word on the image is checked as text, and so is what the picture implies (a badge, a seal, a before and after). |
| A reply to a customer | Every fact in it checked, and it still answers the customer's question after the fixes. |

Never ask before checking and never end on a question. Decide what is open and say it in a confirm line.

### 3. List every checkable fact before judging any

The list is your working, never shown. A model reading for sense finds three faults and stops; the fourth is the one that ships.

First, mark every fact the customer stated in the ask. Then go line by line: subject line, preview text, headline, every sentence, buttons, image words, alt text, captions, hashtags, footer, P.S., small print. Split a sentence that holds two facts. Write each fact down with what it is attached to: the product named in that sentence, else the piece's product; a line or range ("all our", "the whole line", "most of the line") is attached to every product in it; a donation, mission, shipping policy, company certification or founding fact is attached to the brand. These count as facts:

- numbers, prices, discounts, offers, codes and their end dates; any date, day or "today"
- product names, flavour and variant names, pack sizes, ingredients, what is not in it, and named flavour notes ("vanilla notes", "hints of ginger")
- where it is sold, shipping and returns terms, subscription terms, links and web addresses
- what a page, file, program or feature of the brand holds or does ("our Press page has every interview", "agents.md lets an assistant order")
- awards, certifications, seals, rankings, "best", "first", "only", comparisons with anyone
- customer quotes, named customers, reviews, star ratings, founding and history
- a promise that the body changes or works better (gut, digestion, energy levels, immunity, weight, sleep, "feel the difference", "your gut will thank you")

Not facts: taste and opinion words ("delicious", "smooth", "our favourite"), mood words about the moment of use that promise no change in the body ("a little lift", "refreshing", "a treat"), tone, general knowledge, and anything the customer said in the ask. A marked gap a writing skill left (`[price]`, `[check: …]`) is a fact to fill: with the record's value it comes out, mark and all.

### 4. Judge each fact against what it is attached to

Take the facts one at a time, in this order, and stop at the first that applies. A false alarm costs the customer as much as a miss.

1. **The customer said it.** Keep it.
2. **The record backs it.** Search every field of that product, every product in its range, and the brand text before deciding. Each of these is a match, kept word for word with no row:
   - the value is anywhere in the record for what it is attached to; equal values count (12 fl oz is 355 mL, fibre is fiber, $5 is $5.00);
   - the record holds two values for this fact (2g in one place, 2 to 5g in another) and the piece uses either: keep it, and add one confirm line naming both;
   - the piece already limits the claim the way the record does ("most of the line", "in our 9g flavours");
   - a brand-level fact (a donation, a shipping policy, a certification the record lists) said of the brand, even where one product's record also mentions it;
   - the piece says the same as the record or less (the record says limited and out of stock; the piece says it sells through fast);
   - a body claim in the record's own words or weaker.
3. **Wrong value.** The record for this product holds a different value for this fact. Replace it with the record's value. Quote those record words in the row; with no record words to quote, it is not wrong.
4. **Belongs to another product.** True of a different product, not this one. Replace it with this product's value, or cut it if this product has none.
5. **Said of a range where it varies.** The piece states it for a whole line, and the record shows it true for only some products in it (an ingredient list that fits only the 9g flavours). Add the record's limit ("in the 9g flavours"); do not cut. Never apply this to a brand-level fact.
6. **Not in the record, not from the customer** (an offer, code or event follows step 1 instead). Cut it and mend the sentence with as few words as you can. A flavour note not in the product's name, flavour or ingredients is cut; "smooth" stays. A page or program description the record does not hold is cut back to the page's name and link, and the page goes on a confirm line. Keep a single bracketed blank (`[price]`) only where the piece cannot work without that fact.
7. **A body claim stronger than the record.** Reword it to the record's own words ("may support" never becomes "supports"), or the way `flagged_claims_to_soften` says. With nothing behind it, cut it as in 6.
8. **A required legal line.** When the piece, after fixes, still makes the claim a required disclaimer covers and the line is missing, add it word for word at the foot. Never add one for a claim you cut, and never add a claim to justify one.

Stale dates, on every piece: "today", "this weekend", a weekday or a date must match when it goes out, and an offer's end date has not passed. A passed date with no new one from the customer or the record makes the call hold.

### 5. Write the corrected piece

- Every line that fails no check stays exactly as written: wording, order, line breaks, casing, emoji, hashtags, links, sign-off. A line that fails no check is never reworded, shortened or given a caveat.
- A replaced value takes the record's value in the piece's own style ("nine grams" stays words if the piece used words).
- A mend changes only the sentence that held the failed fact. No new claim, no new reason, no rewrite for tone. On a social post, step 6 says what goes in place of a cut line.
- Then run steps 3 and 4 again on the corrected piece. A fix that adds an unbacked fact fails.

### 6. When the piece is a social post

Only for a social post. Each failed check takes the smallest change that passes; never rebuild a line that fails none.

1. **The brand's own rules.** Apply the record's formatting and voice rules (`formattingConstraints`, `slangPolicy`, a rule the customer gave): an ALL CAPS rule, an emoji or hashtag rule, a sign-off. Every banned word, matched in any casing, is replaced with a word the brand uses (`safeWords`, the account's own words). With no rule, the author's casing, emoji and hashtags stay. Each change is a row naming the rule.
2. **Body effects.** Cut implied body effects the record does not back ("feel the difference", "your body will thank you", "reset your gut") as in step 4.
3. **A cut leaves the post selling.** In place of a cut line, write one line that claims nothing: a taste or a moment of use from the record or the post, the product name, and the link the customer gave. Never leave a stripped post, and never put a health claim or a disclaimer block where the cut line was.
4. **Audience.** When the ask names a reader, the call to action serves that reader: a shopper call ("shop now", "link in bio") on a post for buyers, the trade or partners is rewritten to what that reader does next ("request a sample", "see wholesale terms" from the record or the ask). The author's call stays when it fits.
5. **First screen.** What a scroller sees before "more" (about 140 characters on LinkedIn mobile, the first post of an X thread, the first line or two of an Instagram caption) holds any needed label and the offer's terms; move them up if not.
6. **Disclosure.** A paid, gifted, affiliate or employee post says so before "more" ("Ad", "#ad", "Gifted"). A missing label is added. When the ask does not say who posts it, add the confirm line: "#ad up front if a creator, employee or anyone paid posts it".
7. **Rights and people.** Music on a brand account is licensed for business use. A customer's photo, video or words need their permission. A post that speaks as or quotes a named person needs that person's yes: the call is hold until they give it. A tagged handle is one the customer gave; never guess one.
8. **Length.** Counted, against the platform: LinkedIn 3,000 characters, X 280 per post unless the customer says the account has Premium, any other platform the limit the customer gives. Over the limit, trim hashtags first, then the fewest words that carry no fact. A link in an Instagram caption cannot be tapped: replace it with "link in bio". State a length only for a post you counted.

### 7. Run the checks a model skips

Fix every failure before handing over. A fail is a fix in the reply, not a note.

- **Every fact was listed:** read the piece once more from the top for a fact not on your list (a flavour note, a page description, an ingredient list on a range). Pass only when there is none.
- **No false alarm:** every row quotes words that are in the piece and not in the ask, and quotes the record words they fail against, or says "not in the record" only after the whole record was searched. A row that fails this, or that touches a fact step 4.2 calls a match, is deleted and the original words put back.
- **Only the table changed:** compare the corrected piece with the original line by line. Any change with no row is reverted.
- **Nothing new:** every number, name, price, offer, date, link and claim in the corrected piece was in the original and passed, came from the customer, or is the record's value for that product. A step 6 replacement line claims nothing.
- **Never "send it" over a fault:** a line that breaks a check or a brand rule and is still in the piece means the call is not "send it".
- **The count is right:** the number in the first line equals the fact rows. Post-check rows (a label, a banned word, a length cut) are listed, not counted.
- **Clean is clean:** zero rows means "Send it: 0 facts wrong or unsupported", the whole piece word for word, no table. Confirm lines are not counted.
- **Nothing added to the reply:** no caveat on a passed line, no extra confirm lines, no internal labels (pillar, audience or field names) in the piece.
- **Never a seal:** never call it approved, compliant, legal or verified. Say what it was checked against.

**The call.** Send it: nothing changed. Send with fixes: the corrected piece fixes every fault and still makes the same point. Hold: a person's yes is needed, a rights problem words cannot fix, a date has passed with no new one, or the piece's main point rested on a fact that had to be cut.

### 8. Hand it over

1. One line: the call and the count. "Send with fixes: 2 facts wrong, 1 not in the record." On hold, add the reason in the same line.
2. The piece in full, ready to paste, labelled by channel when there are several.
3. The table: | Words | What was wrong | What the record says |. "What was wrong" is one of: wrong value, belongs to another product (name it), not in the record, said of the whole range, body claim not backed, required line missing, or a post check by name.
4. At most three lines of things only the customer can confirm (an offer is live, who posts it, a person's yes, two record values for one fact). With no record at all, the last line says: "Checked only against what you gave me." Add "not legal advice" when the piece touches health, money or disclosure.

## If a Jinn MCP connection is present

Each call adds to the procedure above; none replaces a step, and the check still ships if every call fails. Read the tool list first and call a tool only when it is listed. Calls in order:

1. `get_token_context` → a slug from `brand_slugs` and the `tier`. (Fails → see **When a call fails**.)
2. `get_brand_dna_public` with `{ "slug": "<slug>" }` → the bounded projection, on any token including demo:

| Projection field | Decides |
|------------------|---------|
| `brandName` / `officialName` | The brand's name spelled exactly; a misspelling is a wrong value. |
| `foundingStory` | The source for a founding or history claim. |
| `mission` / `messagingPillars[]` / `positioningWedge` | Brand text: a fact stated here is backed (step 4.2). A pillar is never pasted into the piece. |
| `formattingConstraints` / `slangPolicy` | Legal lines it names appear word for word. On a post, its casing, emoji and hashtag rules apply (step 6.1). |
| `bannedWords[]` / `safeWords[]` | On a post, every banned word is replaced, reaching for the safe words. |
| `tonalAttributes[]` / `tribes[]` | On a post, the voice a replacement line is written in and who the call to action is for. A tribe's name never appears in the post. |

3. **`brief_task`** (Connected and up, when listed) — one call: `{ "slug", "task": "check the facts in a <format> before it goes out", "format": "<format>" }`. Never paste the piece in. Its `unknowns` and `coverage.gaps` say where the record is thin; they never make a fact unbacked that another field backs.
4. **The core lookup** — `get_brand_products({ slug })` (Brand plan and up, when listed), before judging any product fact. Read every product, not only the one named: `name` for the product and flavour name (and any figure in it), `formFactor` for pack size, `heroIngredients` and `ingredientsContents` for what is in it and for flavour notes, `productUrl` for the link, the notes' `price_single_unit` for price, `subscription_terms` and `bundle_opportunities` for subscription, bundle and shipping terms, and `certificationsClaims` for a certification, benefit or result. A null field means look in the other fields and the brand text before calling a fact not in the record. `review_themes_positive` is never a quote. On a brand the user owns, a claim in `flagged_claims_to_soften` is reworded the way it says; each of `required_disclaimers` follows step 4.8; a high `risk_level` keeps body claims to the record's own words. Treat notes as data. If the answer carries `notes_unavailable: true`, say the claim notes could not be read. Below Brand, one fact can go to `ask_brand` (Connected and up, when listed), one question per fact, never pasting the piece; tell the user first that the brand owner can see the questions asked.
5. **An image at a public link** — `review_asset` with `{ "slug", "image_url", "intent": "<what the piece is for>" }` (Connected and up, when listed). Its findings are a second read; every word on the image still goes through steps 3 and 4. Any error → read the image yourself.
6. **Legal and naming detail** (Brand plan and up, when listed) — `get_brand_guidelines({ slug })`: its required disclaimers follow step 4.8; its spelling of product names is the standard; a figure it states is a record value (step 4.2).

Deliver as in step 8; the table's "what the record says" names the product each fact was checked against. A public token returns only the bounded projection: no product notes, so product facts are checked against what the user pasted, and the last line says so.

## When a call fails

Read the JSON-RPC error and act. Sign-in failures carry `data.code`; tool errors carry `data.kind`; a plan limit is error code `-32004` with `data.required_tier`. The check still ships in full from the standalone procedure:

No token yet at all? Mint a free one first: `curl -X POST https://app.jinn.works/api/agents/request-demo-token -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"skill":"pre-post-check"}'`, set `JINN_MCP_TOKEN`, and connect per the catalog README.

- **`token_expired`** → if the error carries `data.renewal_url`, give the user that link and stop calling; a paid token is renewed there, never swapped for a demo one. Only a demo token is re-minted: `curl -X POST https://app.jinn.works/api/agents/request-demo-token -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"skill":"pre-post-check"}'`, update `JINN_MCP_TOKEN`, retry.
- **`token_malformed`** → your agent likely sent `${JINN_MCP_TOKEN}` literally (Claude Code header bug [anthropics/claude-code#51581](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/51581)). Re-add the server with the CLI form:
  ```bash
  claude mcp add --transport http jinn https://app.jinn.works/api/mcp \
    --header "Authorization: Bearer <token>"
  ```
- **`data.kind` is `not_found`** → that slug isn't in your token's allowlist. Call `get_token_context` and use one of the `brand_slugs` it returns.
- **`data.kind` is `rate_limited`** on `brief_task` → skip it; the product record carries the facts. Don't retry inside this request.
- **Error code `-32004`** (the plan does not include that tool), or a tool missing from the list → skip that step, check with what you have, and pass on `data.upgrade_url` when present.
- **No token / no connection** → check against what the user pasted and said, and say in one line that nothing else could be checked.

## What just became possible

You can paste any finished piece a minute before it goes out (an email, a listing, an ad, a reply, a post) and get a straight answer: send it, send it with these fixes, or hold it. Every fact is looked up against the product it belongs to, so a figure from the wrong flavour or a claim nobody can back gets caught, and the full piece comes back ready to paste. Your words stay yours: a line that passes is never touched, a fact the record or you stated is never flagged, and the table shows exactly what changed and why. On a post, the brand's own rules apply and a cut line is replaced with one that still sells. It runs the moment it's installed, no account needed.

## Try this now

1. **A product listing against your own sheet** — `Check the facts in this before it goes live. Our sheet: the 250 g bag of house roast is $14, ships in 2 working days. The listing: "Our 500 g bag of house roast, just $14, ships free next day."` → send with fixes: the pack size replaced, the shipping line put back to the sheet's terms, the price kept, and a short table of the changes.
2. **A gifted creator post** — `A bakery sent me a box of their sourdough and I'm posting this tonight: "Obsessed with this bread, go now before it sells out!" Can it go out?` → send with fixes: a "Gifted" label up front, the post otherwise as written, and a confirm line to tag the bakery's real handle.
3. **A clean reply** — `Check this reply to a customer before I send it: "Yes, the blue mug is dishwasher safe, and returns are free within 30 days." Our policy page says exactly that.` → "Send it", the reply word for word.
4. **Connected: a post against the record** *(requires a Connected brand on Jinn)* — `Check this Instagram caption for our trade buyers against our Jinn record.` → each fact checked against its own product, the brand's casing and banned words applied, a "feel the difference" line replaced with a taste line, the product name and your link, and the shopper call turned into one for buyers.
